# Defect Priority Summary

## Purpose

This document shows how defect information can be summarised in a stakeholder-friendly way.

The focus is not on listing every issue in technical detail, but on explaining business impact and decision relevance.

---

## Example defect themes from portfolio-style coverage

### High importance examples

#### Reporting mismatch between dashboard and exported data
**Why it matters:**  
Reduces trust in stakeholder-facing outputs and may lead to incorrect business decisions.

**Typical impact:**  
Incorrect totals, inconsistent revenue reporting, loss of confidence in dashboard accuracy.

**Suggested priority:**  
High

---

#### Unauthorised access to export capability
**Why it matters:**  
Could expose data to users who should not have access.

**Typical impact:**  
Security/compliance concern, role-control failure, governance issue.

**Suggested priority:**  
High

---

### Medium importance examples

#### Locked-out user messaging unclear or inconsistent
**Why it matters:**  
Affects user understanding and support experience, but may not block core processing.

**Typical impact:**  
Poor user experience, avoidable support queries.

**Suggested priority:**  
Medium

---

#### Responsive layout issue on smaller viewport
**Why it matters:**  
Affects usability on smaller screens but may not stop core functionality.

**Typical impact:**  
Reduced accessibility/usability, visual confusion.

**Suggested priority:**  
Medium

---

### Lower importance examples

#### Minor wording inconsistency in dashboard metadata
**Why it matters:**  
Low business impact unless it causes confusion in reporting interpretation.

**Typical impact:**  
Presentation issue only.

**Suggested priority:**  
Low

---

## Stakeholder takeaway

The most important defects are not always the most technical ones.

In assurance-led QA, priority should reflect:

- business impact
- user impact
- data trust
- release confidence
- governance or control concerns
